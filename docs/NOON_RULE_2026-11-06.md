# Noon price-rise rule (R10): Germany, Austria, and neighbours

Research date: 2026-11-06. Purpose: decide what the app may say and do for R10 (arrival-time-aware price labels and the "fill before noon" hint). All cent figures below are quoted from the named source and attributed; none are computed by us.

Legend: [opened] = page fetched and read in this session. [snippet] = only seen in a search result summary, not opened. [pdf-text] = official PDF downloaded and text extracted locally.

## 1. Germany

### Rule
- Law: Kraftstoffpreisanpassungsgesetz (KPAnG), Article 1 of the "Kraftstoffmassnahmenpaket", BGBl. 2026 I Nr. 82, issued 31 March 2026, dated 27 March 2026. [pdf-text, recht.bund.de]
- Section 2(1): operators of public fuel stations that set their own prices, and companies that dictate pump prices to such operators, "may raise these prices only once per calendar day, at 12 o'clock" (12 Uhr). Price decreases are not restricted by the text, so they are allowed at any time and any number of times.
- Section 1: "fuels" means all petrol and diesel fuels (not only E5/E10/diesel as in the cabinet draft). Public stations as defined in GWB section 47k(3) sentence 2.
- Section 2(2): the federal government may, by ordinance (needs Bundesrat and Bundestag consent), specify details "including a suspension" of the duty. No such ordinance was found.
- Section 3: intentional or negligent increase against section 2(1) is an administrative offence; fine up to 100,000 EUR.
- Section 4: the Federal Ministry for Economics and Energy reports to the legislature one year after entry into force.
- Article 3: in force the day after promulgation, i.e. 1 April 2026 (Bundeskartellamt confirms 1 April 2026).

### Sunset
- The official text contains no expiry date. Only the one-year evaluation duty (section 4). The Bundestag page says the measure is "initially time-limited and evaluated after one year" [opened]; this is not supported by the BGBl text, so we treat it as: in force, no automatic expiry, evaluation due about April 2027.
- Political status (as of September 2026): ADAC calls for abolition; Schleswig-Holstein proposed abolition in the Bundesrat (agenda reported 23 Sep 2026, outcome not found). [opened: autohaus.de] No amendment, suspension or repeal found in searches [snippet]. The rule could therefore change; the app must not hard-code permanence.

### Exceptions and fuzziness
- Bundeskartellamt: the KPAnG does not say whether a noon increase is allowed during a station's closing hours; state authorities interpret. No formal tolerance window; nearly 90% of recorded deviations fall between 11:50 and 12:10, many looking like technical start-up issues. [opened]
- Enforcement is by the Laender (fines), the Markttransparenzstelle (MTS-K) at the Bundeskartellamt monitors automatically. Reported: "thousands" of deviations since April, including gross ones early on; no count of confirmed violations. [opened: Bundeskartellamt; autohaus.de]
- Motorway stations are covered by the law (no exemption in the text).

### Evidence on actual patterns (secondary sources, all with caveats)
- ADAC, analysis of over 14,000 stations, May 2026: cheapest shortly before 12:00 (about 3.7 ct/l below the daily average for E10, 4.3 ct/l for diesel); the noon jump averages 14.6 ct/l (E10) and 18.4 ct/l (diesel); prices fall gradually after noon, below the daily average from about 17:30; small rises around 22:00 and 24:00 as some cheap stations close; prices fall from about 05:00 until noon. Before April: daily swing about 13 ct/l, highest 07-08, lowest 19-20. [opened: adac.de, June 2026]
- ADAC, September 2026: 17 ct gap between daily high and low for diesel; ADAC says the intraday price gap widened and overall prices did not fall. [opened: autohaus.de]
- Shiftmove (fleet telematics) via AUTO BILD, 22 Oct 2026: 90 days at 15-minute resolution, stations away from motorways, 181,620 fleet fuelling transactions; average daily low about 10:15 (diesel) and 10:30 (petrol); afternoon prices higher than morning on all days; reported swing 14.6 ct/l diesel, 10.8 ct/l petrol; best window 9:45 to 11:45. Article is internally inconsistent (14.5 vs 14.6 ct) and the original study was not seen. [opened: autobild.de]
- Bundeskartellamt describes (not measured) a jump around 12:00 "sometimes about 10 cents" and says it is too early to judge a price effect. [opened]
- Auto Motor und Sport gives a time-of-day sketch without data or source; do not cite as evidence. [opened]
- Disagreement to keep in mind: ADAC (May) puts the low "shortly before 12:00"; Shiftmove (non-motorway, fleets) puts the average low near 10:15-10:30 with 9:45-11:45 as window. Both agree: before noon is cheaper than after noon. The exact minute differs by method, so the app should say "before 12:00", not a minute.

## 2. Austria

### Rule
- Regulation: "Standesregeln fuer Tankstellenbetreiber ueber den Zeitpunkt der Preisauszeichnung fuer Treibstoffe bei Tankstellen" (Spritpreisverordnung), Stammfassung BGBl. II Nr. 484/2010; amendments incl. 186/2012, 75/2013, 471/2013, 399/2016, 412/2019, 492/2022, 320/2025, 48/2026. [opened: RIS, consolidated version of 06.11.2026, Gesetzesnummer 20007082]
- Section 1: "A price increase is permitted every day only at 12.00" (Eine Preiserhoehung ist an jedem Tag nur um 12.00 Uhr zulaessig). The changeover must happen without delay as far as the existing technical equipment allows. Price decreases may be posted at any time.
- Section 2: in force since 1 January 2011; expires at the end of 31 December 2028.
- So Austria has had a once-daily noon rise for about 15 years. The model Germany copied.

### 2026 episode
- Section 1a (BGBl. II Nr. 48/2026): a stricter version allowing increases only Monday, Wednesday and Friday at 12:00, valid 16 March 2026 to the end of 12 April 2026. It has lapsed; the daily noon rule applies again since 13 April 2026. [opened: RIS 20007082 and WKO page]
- The earlier "three increases per day (9, 13, 17)" idea is not Austrian law: no source found. Discard it.

### Tolerance and enforcement
- WKO reading of the ministry: changeover starts at 12:00; a manual changeover takes about 5 to 10 minutes; operators should document longer changeovers, in which case no offence exists. [opened: wko.at, page dated about April 2026]
- Penalties: Gewerbeordnung section 367 Z 22 up to 2,180 EUR; Preisauszeichnungsgesetz section 15(1) up to 1,450 EUR. [opened: wko.at]
- Separate: E-Control price database reporting under the Preistransparenzverordnung Treibstoffpreise 2011 (report within 30 minutes of a change; a new price set at 12:00 must be reported immediately; expires 31 Dec 2028). [opened: RIS 20007391] Relevant to data freshness for Austria, not to the noon rule itself.

### Evidence on patterns
- No Austrian measured intraday data was found in this research. Do not claim Austrian cent figures.

## 3. Slovenia, Croatia and others (different mechanism, not a noon rule)
- Slovenia: government-regulated model prices for fuel at stations outside motorways and expressways (gov.si [opened]). Uradni list RS 945/2026 (12 June 2026) extended the regulation six months [snippet: tax-fin-lex/siol]; reported to include motorway stations from June 2026 [snippet, conflicts with gov.si page wording, unverified]. Weekly cycle from 31 March 2026 (7-day averages) [snippet]; gov.si news items dated 19 Oct, 26 Oct, 2 Nov 2026 are all Mondays announcing "from tomorrow" (Tuesday). [opened] Conclusion: weekly Tuesday change, no intraday rule.
- Croatia: prices set weekly by government decision on Monday, valid from Tuesday 00:00 for seven days [opened: nafta.hr, 16 Oct 2026]. No decree number was shown; no intraday rule.
- Implication for the app: for HR and SI, the relevant time effect is the weekly Tuesday step, not noon. A "prices change on Tuesday" hint is possible but is a different feature (outside R10).
- Other countries: not researched in depth. No other noon-type rule was found; do not extend the noon logic to any other country without a source.

## 4. How a driver should act
- Both countries: raises happen only at 12:00; so a price seen before noon cannot have risen since the previous noon (it may have fallen). After noon, expect the day's high and a gradual fall. Fill before 12:00 where the route allows; avoid arriving 12:00 to about 13:00. [logical consequence of the legal text; the sizes are from the sources above]
- Not a guarantee: stations may lower prices at any time, may breach the rule (Germany: thousands of reported deviations), and the German rule may be changed or abolished.
- Do not tell drivers to wait until after noon to fill: no source supports it for a price benefit. Late evening can also be fine (ADAC) but with no reliable daily low (Auto Motor und Sport, unsourced), so keep the hint to "before 12:00".

## 6. Wording the app may use (EN / SV), without overclaiming
- Where DE/AT, price observed after the last 12:00 and before the next: "Posted price. In Germany and Austria prices can only rise at 12:00, so this price is the same or lower than when it was last posted." Hold to the "at most" label only if the observation is not older than the last noon and the station is not suspected of breaching the rule.
- Observed before the last noon, now after: "Price may have risen at 12:00."
- Hint: "Prices in Germany and Austria can only rise at 12:00 each day. Fuelling before noon is usually cheaper than just after." (use "usually", not a number)
- Staleness: "valid until 12:00" per country, not a flat 10 hours.
- SV: "Priserna i Tyskland och Oesterrike kan bara hoejas klockan 12.00. Att tanka fore klockan 12 ar oftast billigare an strax efter." (write with correct a/o diacritics in the app strings.)
- Do not write: any cent amount as a fact of the app, "cheapest at 10:15", "the rule is guaranteed", "stations always comply", "rule applies in Slovenia/Croatia", "three price rises per day".
- If the app ever shows a cent figure, show it as "ADAC, May 2026: on average about 14.6 ct/l (E10) higher after noon than before" with source and date, never as our own estimate. Preference: show none for now.
- DE legal-risk note for README/docs: the rule is law today, but under political review; the config switch above is the safety valve.
